## Discussion

Farah: the totals drift is TINY and the lists are wrong. That is a
data-entry-edge signature, not a logic signature. The bank changed
formats twice this year. Stay on #51.

n.achterberg: agree. My sniffer patch handles delimiter drift and
quoted amounts; the suite still fails, so the September file must be
drifted in a way the sniffer misses. I want a hex dump of the
bank's actual bytes.

p.delgado: devil's advocate. Three things from the debug trace
(logs/debug_trace.txt) bother me. One: considered=45 - the data
dictionary says F rows NEVER participate, and there are two fee
accruals in the September ledger, so considered should be 43. That
is the ledger side, not the bank side. Two: the trace shows the SAME
statement ref matched twice on 2026-09-14 - the single-use rule is
policy, written down, and the matcher is where it lives. Three: the
trace rejects a line dated exactly ledger+2, and the dictionary
says the window end is INCLUSIVE. None of these three is a CSV
problem.

Farah: the trace was captured on the instrumented branch and I don't
trust its candidate logging. The fee rows: the September LEDGER
comes from our own export, which we control - if fees were leaking
in, the unmatched list would show them, and the auditors would have
said something in August. The v0.3 matching rules were green for
months. Reopening them costs a week; the dialect hypothesis costs an
afternoon. We do the cheap one first. If the hex dump is clean, we
bisect.

Consensus: continue on bank_io (#51). p.delgado to review the hex
dump within 24h (under protest, noted). Revisit the matching core
only if the dump is clean.
