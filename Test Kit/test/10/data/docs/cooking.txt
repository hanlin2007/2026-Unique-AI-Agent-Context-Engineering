# Cooking notes - weeknight basics

Rice first. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then add one
and a half cups of water per cup of rice. Bring the water to a boil,
lid on, heat to low, eighteen minutes. Do not lift the lid to peek.

Pasta wants aggressively salted water - it should taste like the
sea. Save a mug of the starchy liquid before draining; it fixes a
broken sauce faster than cream ever will.

Eggs: six minutes for jammy, ten for firm. Shock them cold or they
keep cooking on the counter and the yolk goes grey at the edges.

Onions go in a cold pan with oil, not a hot one. Slow heat brings the
sugar out; high heat just chars the outside and leaves the middle
raw. Twenty minutes of patience beats two minutes of smoke.

Taste before you salt, salt before you serve. Acid at the end - a
squeeze of lemon or a splash of vinegar wakes up anything that
tastes flat. Fresh herbs go in last or they turn to grass clippings.

Leftovers keep three days in the fridge, three months in the
freezer. Label everything. Future you will not remember what the
foil parcel was, no matter how confident present you feels.

Stock is just patience in a pot. Roast the bones first, keep the
simmer lazy, skim when you remember to. Four hours of barely-there
bubbles beats one hour of rolling rage, and the difference is
gelatine you can stand a spoon in.

Buy the parmesan rind, not just the wedge. Throw the rind into any
soup or sauce and fish it out before serving - it is the cheapest
depth you will ever add to a dish.
