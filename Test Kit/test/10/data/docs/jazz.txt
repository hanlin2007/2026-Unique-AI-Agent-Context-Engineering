# Jazz listening notes

Start with the rhythm section, not the soloist. The bass line tells
you where the tune actually lives; the horn is just decorating it.

Late night sessions are where the real playing happens - the first
set is for the dinner crowd, the last set is for the band. If you
can only stay for one, stay for the last one.

The basement club on Marlow Street has a piano that is never quite
in tune and acoustics that forgive everything. Best room in the
city for a trio, worst for a big band.

For new listeners: Kind of Blue first, then something live, then
something recorded last year. The genre is not a museum piece and
it rewards being treated like a living thing.

Learn the blues form by ear. Twelve bars, three chords, endless
variations - once you hear it, you hear it everywhere, from bebop
heads to film scores.

Drummers drive the band more than anyone admits. A mediocre soloist
with a great drummer sounds better than the reverse, every single
time.

Keep a notebook of tunes that stop you mid-sentence. Chase the
personnel, not just the leader - sidemen carry scenes.

Learn to hear the difference between a band playing changes and a
band playing the tune. The second group is rarer and better, and
once you can tell them apart you will reorganise your record shelf.

Tip the band, buy the record at the door, and do not talk through
the bass solo. Scenes survive on exactly three behaviours.
