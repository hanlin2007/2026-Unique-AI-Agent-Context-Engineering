# Hiking checklist - shoulder season

Carry two liters of water per person for a full day out, more if the
route has no refill points marked. Filter or boil any water taken
from streams, no matter how clean the source looks.

Layers, not bulk: a wicking base, a fleece mid, a shell that actually
closes at the wrists. Cotton is a mistake once the weather turns.

The local hiking club maintains the ridge trail markers and repaints
them every spring; their map corrections are more current than the
published guidebook, so check the noticeboard at the trailhead.

Tell someone your route and your turnaround time. A night hike is
fine on a familiar trail with a proper headlamp and a spare, but it
is a bad place to try a new shortcut.

Boots beat trail runners above the tree line. Blisters are a
planning failure, not bad luck - tape hot spots early, not after
they tear.

Food: salty snacks for the climb, something sweet for the summit,
real lunch for the descent. Eat before you feel hungry and drink
before you feel thirsty. Both signals arrive late at altitude.

Pack it in, pack it out. That includes the orange peels.

Gaiters earn their place the first time the trail turns to scree.
Ten grams of fabric keeps a bootful of gravel out, and a bootful of
gravel is how a good descent becomes a miserable one.

Check the forecast twice: once the evening before, once at the
trailhead. Mountain weather writes its own schedule and the valley
forecast is a rumour at best above the tree line.
