# Astronomy log - autumn

The night sky was clear for the equinox session, first proper clear
stretch in three weeks. Transparency good, seeing mediocre.

Jupiter is the evening object right now. At sixty magnification the
two main belts are easy; at a hundred and fifty the Great Red Spot
shows as a pale dimple when it rotates into view.

Night after night the moon climbs later, which is exactly what the
deep-sky list wants. New moon weekend is booked for the dark-site
trip if the forecast holds.

The sky darkens properly after half ten this month. Arcturus is
setting early in the west and the summer triangle is sliding toward
the horizon - the season is turning over.

Log the sketch before the eyepiece fogs, not after. Memory flatters
every observation by about twenty percent.

Collimation check before every session. Two minutes with the laser
tool saves an evening of blaming the optics for what the mirror
alignment did.

Binoculars first for beginners, always. A cheap scope on a wobbly
mount kills more interest than cloud ever did. Learn the bright
constellations with nothing at all, then add glass.

Dew is the real enemy, not cold. A cheap heater band on the finder
saves the session; the main objective fogs last and forgives most.
Bring twice the layers you think you need - standing still at an
eyepiece generates no heat at all.

Keep the red torch dim and the phone in the pocket. Adaptation takes
twenty minutes to build and one white screen to destroy.
