# Gardening journal - September

The tomatoes are finishing. Water deeply once a week rather than a
little every day - shallow sips teach roots to stay near the surface
where the heat kills them.

Water the tomatoes at the base, never over the leaves. Wet foliage
in a cool September is an engraved invitation to blight.

Compost turning day. The heap should feel like a wrung-out sponge;
if it steams, you got the ratio right. Too dry and nothing happens,
too wet and it sulks into sludge.

Sow the overwintering broad beans this month, two fingers deep,
eight inches apart. The mice know the schedule too, so net the bed
until the shoots are a hand tall.

Rain barrel water is fine for the brassicas but the seedlings still
get the can from the tap - the barrel sits in full sun and a warm
shock checks young roots.

Order spring bulbs now or accept the dregs in November. Plant them
twice as deep as they are tall, pointy end up, and then forget them
entirely until March.

Last feed of the year for the pots. Anything given nitrogen after
the equinox grows soft and the first frost takes it.

Lift the dahlias after the first blackening frost, dry them upside
down in the shed, and pack them in dry compost. Label the varieties
now; in April one tuber looks exactly like another.

Leave the seed heads on the teasels and the sunflowers. The
goldfinches will repay the favour all winter, and the garden looks
better with a bit of structure than cut to the ground.
