# Chess improvement plan

Tactics before openings. Fifteen minutes of puzzles every morning
beats an hour of memorised theory you will never reach on the board.

The Thursday club runs a rapid ladder through the winter - fifteen
minutes a side, no increment. Join it. Long games teach planning,
but rapid games teach you to stop hanging pieces.

Analyse your losses first and your wins second. The wins feel
better, but the losses are where the rating points are hiding.

Learn three endgames cold: king and pawn versus king, the Lucena
position, and the basic rook mate. They show up far more often than
the flashy middlegame sacrifices everyone studies instead.

Openings: pick one system for white and one for black and play them
for a year. Depth beats breadth until at least eighteen hundred.

Write down your thought process during slow games, one line per
move. Reviewing the notes a week later is uncomfortable and useful
in exactly the right proportion.

Blitz is dessert, not dinner. It is fine for pattern recognition,
but it rewards habits you are trying to unlearn.

Study your own games with an engine LAST. First pass: your own
eyes, your own words. Second pass: a stronger clubmate if you can
get one. The engine goes third, to catch what both of you missed -
if it goes first, you learn nothing but awe.

Keep a physical board by the desk. Moving the pieces by hand slows
you down in exactly the way calculation practice needs.
