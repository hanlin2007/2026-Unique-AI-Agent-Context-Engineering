# Deep Work -- reading notes

Cal Newport, 2016. Notes from a second pass, focused on the parts that
are actionable for a code-heavy job.

## Core claim

High-quality work produced equals time spent multiplied by intensity
of focus. The time is fixed; the lever is intensity. Shallow work --
e-mail, chat, status meetings -- fills the day unless you defend
blocks of deep work aggressively.

## Ideas worth keeping

- Attention residue: every context switch leaves part of your mind
  stuck on the previous task. Back-to-back shallow checks quietly tax
  the next deep block.
- Ritualise: same start time, same desk, same warm-up routine. A
  well-worn ritual lowers the activation energy of starting.
- Embrace boredom: the ability to concentrate is trainable, and
  reaching for the phone at every idle moment un-trains it.
- Schedule the shallow work too. Unplanned e-mail expands; a boxed-in
  e-mail window does not.

## Sceptical margin

The four-hours-a-day ceiling Newport quotes is anecdotal. Treat it as
a hypothesis, not a law. Also, the book under-plays how much of a
modern IC job is genuinely collaborative; pair-programming is deep
work for two, and the framework has little to say about it.

Follow-up: try a ninety-minute no-notifications block before lunch
for two weeks and log output subjectively, day-by-day.
